00 — CONTEXT

How a warranty claim reaches a company that never sold the car

Autoliv does not sell vehicles. It sells airbags, seatbelts, steering wheels and inflators to the companies that do. So when a safety part fails in the field, Autoliv is the last party to hear about it — and the first to be charged for it.

The chain runs like this, and every step of it happens before Autoliv is involved at all.

01
A part fails

A driver notices a warning lamp, a noise, a belt that will not retract.

02
A dealer repairs it

Under the vehicle warranty, at no cost to the driver. The dealer raises a repair order.

03
The OEM pays the dealer

Parts and labour. The claim is coded to a labour operation and a part number.

04
The OEM charges Autoliv

A debit memo against the supplier whose part is on the claim. The money is taken, not requested.

05
Autoliv must answer

Inside a response window. Prove the part met specification, or the charge stands.

THE CONSTRAINT THAT SHAPES EVERYTHING

Autoliv never sees the vehicle, the driver, or the repair. Its entire knowledge of the failure is a row of data and, sometimes, a part in a box. Every capability in this document exists to get more out of those two things, faster, than a person reading them one at a time can.

Why the volumes are what they are

Autoliv holds roughly 44% of the global passive-safety market and builds around 143 million seatbelt systems, 143 million side airbags, 61 million frontal airbags and 21 million steering wheels a year, from 62 plants in 23 countries. Its five largest customers — Stellantis, Toyota, Volkswagen, Honda and Ford — are about 44% of sales between them.

At that scale even an excellent defect rate produces a large number of claim rows. And because these are safety parts, the tail risk is severe rather than frequent: the product-related liability reserve sits at $82.0m against $10.8bn of sales — under one percent — but a single field action can carry a disclosed range of $0 to $123m. That combination, high volume and low frequency with severe tails, is why the process below has both an industrial front end and a very carefully gated back end.

THE VOCABULARY, ONCE
Debit memo
The customer charging Autoliv for warranty repairs it attributes to an Autoliv part. Money already deducted from payment, not an invoice awaiting approval. Contesting it has a deadline.
Recovery agreement
The contract that governs what the customer may charge and on what basis. It fixes the coverage period, the look-back limit and the technical factor. Every recovery argument cites a clause of it.
Technical factor
Autoliv's agreed percentage share of a claim's cost. A memo applying 100% where the schedule says 48% is the single most common overcharge.
Look-back
How far back in a vehicle's life the customer may reach. Past that limit — typically 24 or 36 months in service — a charge is not permitted, however genuine the failure.
MIS
Months in service. A vehicle's age at the moment of repair. The axis every warranty rate is measured on.
IPTV
Incidents per thousand vehicles, at a stated MIS band. The industry's core failure-rate measure. Each customer has its own name for it — R/1000 at Ford, C/1000 at Stellantis, 1000 FZG at Volkswagen — which is why the extracts have to be normalised before they can be compared.
3-MIS band
Failures in the first three months. The earliest signal available and the one the customer notices first, because it lands while the vehicle is still new to its owner.
CPV
Cost per vehicle. Total warranty cost divided by vehicles built. The figure that carries an annual reduction target in the customer contract.
NTF
No trouble found. A returned part that meets its full specification on every test. It is a finding, not a shrug — and it is the basis on which a charge can be reversed.
RPA / the bench
Returned-parts analysis. The centres at Ogden, Vårgårda and Hwasung where returned units are torn down, tested and adjudicated. The only place physical evidence exists.
Containment
Stopping suspect product from reaching customers — a lot hold, a 100% re-test at end of line, a market hold. It costs money whether or not the suspicion proves out.
8D
The eight-discipline problem-solving format the industry runs on, D1 to D8. D3 is interim containment, D4 root cause, D5 permanent corrective action, D7 the update to the design and process documents so it cannot recur.
DFMEA / PFMEA
The design and process failure-mode analyses. An 8D that closes without changing one of these has taught the organisation nothing.
Provision
The money set aside in the accounts for warranty and recall exposure. Accrued when a loss is probable and estimable; disclosed but not accrued when it is only reasonably possible.
01 — WHERE THE MONEY LEAKS

Not in the arguments Autoliv loses — in the ones it never gets to make

Across the industry, parts suppliers carry roughly a third of automotive revenue and report roughly a tenth of warranty expense. That gap is not OEM generosity. It is recovery that was available and never claimed.

  • Deadlines beat arguments. A debit memo that breached the recovery agreement becomes a memo Autoliv accepted, simply because the window closed. Screening one memo properly means reading the agreement, the MIS value on every claim line and the settlement history — for one memo, of twenty-two open.
  • Five customers, five dialects. Each publishes claims on its own schedule in its own schema using its own rate metric. Reconciling them to one part-level view is a week of somebody's month, every month — and during that week the data is not being looked at.
  • Evidence is assembled too late. An NTF finding is worth what its evidence is worth. The bench records that support it are pulled together when the customer disputes the disposition, eighteen months after it was made.
  • Coded reasons hide real failures. A noise with no fault code and no reproduction at the workshop codes as "customer concern, no fault found" in every OEM taxonomy — and that code is excluded from the defect curve. Ninety-four of them can describe the same mechanism while the reported rate stays flat.
  • Containment is judged on hindsight. The decision is taken in week one with partial evidence and reviewed in month six with all of it. If what was known at the time was never written down, the reviewer has nothing to judge but the outcome.
  • Provisions rest on unagreed inputs. Cost per vehicle, proportionate responsibility, insurance coverage. A number that averages three unagreed inputs is a guess wearing a measurement's clothes.
02 — THE PROCESS MAP

What the application supports, end to end

Two people carry the record. The warranty analyst holds it from the moment it arrives until it is ready to argue; the warranty manager takes every decision that costs money or changes the product. Everyone else — the bench, the customer quality engineer, the controller, product safety — is called in at a defined point and hands it straight back.

THE ONE THING TO TAKE FROM THE PICTURE

The record moves analyst → manager → analyst → supporting cast → manager and never leaves those two lanes for long. Steps 4, 8 and the two gate branches are the only places it leaves the warranty team, and in each case it comes back. Design for two people and four visitors, not for six equal roles.

03 — THE ELEVEN STEPS

Step by step, with the hand-offs called out

Each step below is a worksheet in the application. Where a capability acts on that step, it is marked on the step rather than listed separately — a feature that cannot be placed on a step in this list is a feature nobody asked for.

ARRIVES AUTOMATICALLY — FIVE CUSTOMER PORTALS, ON THEIR OWN SCHEDULES
1
Normalise the extract, map it to a partWARRANTY ANALYST

A weekly or monthly claim extract lands from a customer portal. Rate metrics are converted to one IPTV series, duplicates removed, and each claim's labour operation resolved to an Autoliv part number. Without this step nothing downstream can be costed, compared or contested.

ADIALECT NORMALISATION AND PART MAPPING

Runs on arrival rather than on a monthly cycle. When a customer renames four labour-operation codes mid-quarter, 185 of 187 claims still resolve on claim-line part number; the rest arrive flagged with the reason they could not, never with a guessed code. A wrong code puts another supplier's cost onto Autoliv's IPTV.

2
Screen the debit memoWARRANTY ANALYST

Each memo is tested against the recovery agreement clause by clause: coverage window, MIS look-back, technical factor, prior settlement, whether the units the charge relies on were actually returned. The output is a position — accept, challenge in part, challenge in full — and the amount contested.

AAGREEMENT SCREENING

Every memo screened, not the largest few. Reading the agreement text against the claim data is what finds the money: on one Honda memo, $487,920 past the 24-MIS look-back, $667,680 from a factor stepped to 100% against a settled 48%, and 412 claims already settled once. Each finding cites the clause it rests on.

3
Match the returned partsWARRANTY ANALYST

Physical units arrive at an analysis centre, often on a pack list with a partial data-matrix read and no claim reference. A part with no claim reference cannot be dispositioned, and the customer's return window does not stop for it.

HANDS OFF → THE BENCH, WITH THE MATCHED UNITS
4
Bench teardown and the NTF callRPA BENCH · SUPPORTING

Teardown, functional test against the specification revision in force, and a finding per unit: meets specification, deviation confirmed, or customer-induced. This is the only physical evidence that will ever exist, and the specification revision it was tested to is part of the finding.

HANDS BACK → WARRANTY ANALYST, WITH THE TEST RECORDS
5
Assemble the caseWARRANTY ANALYST

Claims that share a part and a failure mode become one case. Bench results, dealer narratives, the applicable agreement clause and any prior settlement on the same failure mode are attached, and each evidence finding is attributed: Autoliv responsible, shared or sub-supplier, or not our defect.

AEVIDENCE-LINKED DISPOSITION

The evidence is attached at the moment of the decision rather than reconstructed after it, and a disposition cannot be set without naming what it rests on. Eighteen months later, when the customer reopens it, the argument is still readable — including which four of thirty-eight units passed and were recorded separately rather than folded into the confirmed count.

HANDS OFF → WARRANTY MANAGER, WITH THE CASE AND ITS EVIDENCE
6
DispositionWARRANTY MANAGERGATE

The decision on what Autoliv accepts, and at what technical factor. This is the first point in the process where money is committed rather than measured. Below $25,000 it auto-processes; to $250,000 a named approver confirms; above that it routes to the controller.

GPOLICY GATE — CONFIGURATION, NOT CODE

The bands are a tenant setting a supervisor edits, not a release. A customer changing its policy does not wait for a sprint. The gate also records which threshold caught a case and which role it is waiting on, so a case sitting still has a visible reason.

BRANCH → CONTROLLER, IF THE EXPOSURE IS PRODUCT-RELATED OR ABOVE BAND
—
Provision sign-offCONTROLLER · SUPPORTING

Whether the exposure is accrued or disclosed, and on what basis. Each open input is assessed as probable and estimable, reasonably possible, or remote — and named rather than averaged into a single figure.

AEXPOSURE LEDGER WITH NAMED UNKNOWNS

The agent proposes the movement, names every input still open, and drafts the disclosure note in the wording of the prior filing so the two read consistently. It never posts: the posting is a gated write into the system of record on the controller's confirmation.

HANDS BACK → WARRANTY ANALYST, WITH THE DISPOSITION SET
7
Draft the packetWARRANTY ANALYST

The reversal or appeal document: what Autoliv claims back, what it concedes, on which clause, supported by which prior settlements. Conceding the marginal units explicitly is what makes the rest credible to the customer's engineer.

AREVERSAL AND APPEAL PACKETS

Drafted from the playbook for that denial class, citing the clause and the precedents. The playbook is named on the output, so a supervisor improves the playbook rather than the individual letter — and the final wording stays with the person who signs it.

HANDS OFF → CUSTOMER QUALITY ENGINEER, WHO OWNS THE CUSTOMER CHANNEL
8
File and negotiateCQE · SUPPORTING

Filed through the customer's supplier portal and argued. The CQE holds the relationship, knows what that customer's engineer will accept, and carries the negotiation. The record and its evidence go with the filing.

HANDS BACK → WARRANTY MANAGER, WITH THE CUSTOMER'S REPLY
9
Recovery positionWARRANTY MANAGERGATE

Accept the customer's response, settle at a negotiated factor, or write it off. A write-off is a decision with a reason code, not a status change, and above the band it needs a named approver.

THE SAME CLAIM DATA FEEDS IMPROVEMENT CONTINUOUSLY — NOT ONLY ONCE A CASE CLOSES
10
Signal and containmentWARRANTY MANAGERGATE

Emerging failures are found in the same claim data, by part, plant and build week, in the 3-MIS band. Each build lot is then decided individually: contain, hold and watch, or stand down — with the measure and the customer notification set alongside.

ASIGNAL DETECTION AT 3 MIS

The dealer-concentration and seasonality controls are run before a signal reaches a person, so what arrives is already worth a decision. A median nineteen days ahead of customer notice is the difference between containing 18,600 reachable units at about $41 each and a field action at customer rates.

ANARRATIVE CLUSTERING

The capability with no manual equivalent anywhere in the industry. Dealer free text is read at volume and clustered, so ninety-four narratives describing a ratcheting retractor under a "no fault found" code become a named failure mode instead of a flat claim curve. Clusters are proposed for review, never auto-recoded, and the original claim text is never overwritten.

BRANCH → PRODUCT SAFETY, IF THE MECHANISM COULD BE SAFETY-RELEVANT
—
Safety referralPRODUCT SAFETY · SUPPORTING

A hard gate with no dollar band. Product safety takes the referral or declines it, within an acknowledgement window, and the answer is only possible if the affected population can be defined.

APOPULATION SCOPING FROM TRACEABILITY

Build records, lot genealogy and shipment data resolve into a VIN and lot population inside the clock — 40,400 units across four assembly plants, from four sealed units — with chain of custody preserved on the retained parts.

HANDS BACK → WARRANTY MANAGER, AND ON TO THE PLANT AND THE 8D TEAM
11
8D and the design loopWARRANTY MANAGER

D1 to D8, with the part that usually gets skipped tracked explicitly: D7, the update to the DFMEA, the PFMEA or the control plan. An 8D that closes without changing one of those has taught the organisation nothing, and the same claim will arrive again next year.

ATHE LOOP BACK TO STEP 1

This is the only step that reduces future work rather than processing present work. Currently 67% of closed D7s carry a design or process change against a target of 80% — and that percentage, not the recovery rate, is what decides how much of this process is needed in three years.

THE QUESTION THIS MAP ASSUMES AN ANSWER TO

The map above assumes the analyst carries the record through steps 1 to 7 and hands decisions up, rather than the record changing teams at the intake/case boundary. If Autoliv works it the other way — one team creating and submitting, a separate team handling reconciliation and disputes — the queue design and the role model both change. That single answer is worth settling before the schema is frozen; it is expensive to change afterwards.

05 — HOW IT IS BUILT

Two ways to do the same step, one record of what happened

Every step in the map opens as a worksheet on the right of the screen with an agent on the left. The worksheet is a normal application: fields, options, a submit button. The agent can complete any of it. They write the same values to the same record and the same activity trail, and the choice between them is the user's, step by step, not a setting somebody configures once.

PATH ONE
Do it yourself

Open a finding, read the evidence assembled under it, set the attribution, complete the wrap-up panel, submit. No AI involved in the decision at all. This path has to be good enough to use on its own, because on the day the model is wrong it is the path people fall back to.

PATH TWO
Ask the agent

"Run the validity screen." The agent proposes every finding at once, cites what it read, and waits. Apply or discard. Applying writes exactly the values the controls would have written, and the trail records that the agent proposed it and a named person confirmed it.

Three rules that hold at every step

  • The agent proposes; a person confirms. No agent action changes a record without an explicit apply, and anything with money attached passes the policy gate as well. The agent has no authority band of its own.
  • Unknown and clean do not look the same. A test with no returned units shows as needing review, not as passing. Pending fields say "pending" rather than sitting blank. This sounds small; it is the most common way an internal tool loses the trust of the people using it.
  • One trail, not two. Human and agent write the same shape of record with the actor named. There is no separate, quieter log of what the AI did.

08 — GUARDRAILS

What the agent will not do

A capability list is easy to write. This is the list that decides whether the tool gets used or routed around.

×
It does not decide anything with money attached.

Every proposal waits on an explicit apply, and every submission passes the policy gate at step 6, 9 or 10. The agent has no authority band of its own.

×
It does not post to the ledger.

Provision movements are proposed and drafted. The posting happens in the system of record through a gated write, on the controller's confirmation.

×
It does not answer what it cannot cite.

An answer that cannot be grounded in a knowledge-base passage or a tool result is returned as not found — not improvised, and not hedged into something that reads like an answer.

×
It does not overwrite the original.

Claim narratives, bench records and customer documents are preserved unmodified alongside anything extracted from them, so any extracted field can be checked against its source.

×
It does not train on your data for anyone else.

Claim history trains a model for Autoliv. It is not used to train any general-purpose model and does not leave the tenant for that purpose.

×
It does not fight an experienced engineer.

Where a person overrides the model, the override and its eventual outcome are captured and become training signal. Disagreement is the mechanism, not a failure of it.

ONE EXPECTATION TO SET NOW RATHER THAN AFTER GO-LIVE

The predictive model is advisory and will stay advisory. It ranks a queue and names its drivers; it never blocks a submission, makes a disposition, or overrides a person. If anyone in the organisation is expecting it to auto-decide, that expectation is worth correcting before go-live.

10 — WHAT IT NEEDS FROM AUTOLIV

The knowledge is the product

The interface takes weeks. The material below is what decides whether the agent is useful, and none of it can be sourced externally.

MUST HAVE · STEPS 2, 6, 9
Recovery agreements, per customer

Technical-factor schedules, look-back limits, coverage definitions and NTF clauses. Every money-recovering finding cites one of these documents. Without them, step 2 does not exist.

MUST HAVE · STEPS 5, 10
Claim history with the original outcome intact

Roughly two years across at least two customers, with a spread — paid, short-paid, denied, appealed. Critically: if appeal outcomes overwrite the original decision in the source system, the training label is corrupted before we start.

HIGH VALUE · STEP 7
The traps that are not written down

The phrasings your engineers know reliably trigger a denial, and the arguments that reliably work. Worth more than the documented policy, and almost never a document.

HIGH VALUE · ALL STEPS
Your vocabulary

Labour-operation names, plant abbreviations, the informal names people use out loud. An agent that writes in the company's own language gets read; one that writes in generic English gets ignored.

WHERE TO START

Steps 1 and 2 alone. They need only the claim extracts and the recovery agreements, they sit entirely inside the analyst's own work so nothing depends on another team changing how it operates, and the recovery they find is measurable in one quarter. Everything downstream of step 5 depends on decisions about ownership and gates that are worth taking deliberately rather than early.
